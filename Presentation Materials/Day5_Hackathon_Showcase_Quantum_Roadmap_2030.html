2.2 Checklist Before Submission

  • ✅ Quantum circuit is correct — tested on AerSimulator at minimum
  • ✅ Error mitigation applied if running on real hardware
  • ✅ Classical baseline compared — show what's different vs classical approach
  • ✅ Impact quantified — how many people affected, measurable metric
  • ✅ Code is open source — MIT or Apache 2.0 license in repo
  • ✅ README is complete — installation, usage, results summary
  • ✅ Data attribution — all datasets cited, licences noted
  • ✅ Limitations acknowledged — honest about current hardware constraints

2.3 Sample Winning Project Pattern — Quantum Drug Screening

# Example: Quantum-assisted TB drug screening (simplified)
from qiskit_nature.second_q.drivers import PySCFDriver
from qiskit_algorithms import VQE
from qiskit_algorithms.optimizers import COBYLA
from qiskit.circuit.library import EfficientSU2
from qiskit.primitives import Estimator
from qiskit_nature.second_q.mappers import JordanWignerMapper
from qiskit_nature.second_q.algorithms import GroundStateEigensolver

# Drug candidate: isoniazid (INH) — first-line TB drug
# Simulate interaction with InhA enzyme active site model
# (Simplified: only small fragment, not full protein)

def screen_drug_candidate(smiles: str, energy_threshold: float = -1.5) -> dict:
    """
    Quantum screening pipeline for TB drug candidates.
    Returns: binding feasibility estimate
    """
    # In real system: parse SMILES → PySCF geometry
    # Here: use H2 as proxy for demonstration
    driver = PySCFDriver(atom="H .0 .0 .0; H .0 .0 0.735", basis="sto-3g")
    problem = driver.run()
    
    mapper = JordanWignerMapper()
    ansatz = EfficientSU2(num_qubits=2, reps=2)
    estimator = Estimator()
    vqe = VQE(estimator=estimator, ansatz=ansatz, optimizer=COBYLA())
    solver = GroundStateEigensolver(mapper, vqe)
    result = solver.solve(problem)
    
    ground_energy = result.total_energies[0].real
    is_promising = ground_energy < energy_threshold
    
    return {
        "smiles": smiles,
        "ground_energy_Ha": ground_energy,
        "binding_promising": is_promising,
        "confidence": "NISQ approximation — validate with classical CCSD(T)"
    }

# Screen isoniazid model fragment
result = screen_drug_candidate("CC(=O)Nc1ccccc1", energy_threshold=-1.1)
print(f"Drug screening result: {result}")
print("\nNote: Real application requires full protein active site simulation")
print("This is achievable with 100+ logical qubits (~2030+ fault-tolerant device)")

📜 5. Quantum for Humanity Manifesto

On Day 5, Quantum for Humanity Org launches its founding manifesto — a shared commitment to open, inclusive, and ethical quantum science.

The Quantum for Humanity Manifesto (2026)

  1. Open Science: All quantum research funded for humanitarian purposes must be published as open access. No paywalls for humanity's most urgent problems.
  2. Inclusive Access: Quantum computing tools and training must be available without cost to researchers and students in low- and middle-income countries.
  3. Community Sovereignty: Communities most affected by the problems we solve must define the problem statements, not just receive the solutions.
  4. Transparency: Quantum AI and optimisation systems used in public services (healthcare, welfare, criminal justice) must be explainable and auditable.
  5. Non-Weaponisation: This community will not knowingly contribute quantum computing capabilities to weapons of mass destruction or mass surveillance systems.
  6. Intergenerational Equity: Quantum benefits must be distributed across generations. The power to cure, feed, and protect must not be concentrated in the hands of the few.
  7. Cultural Respect: Quantum knowledge builds on centuries of global scientific tradition. We acknowledge all contributors and resist erasure of non-Western contributions to science.

🗺 6. IBM Quantum Roadmap 2026–2033 — Complete Picture

2023
Quantum Utility Demonstrated
IBM Heron r1 (133Q), Eagle 127Q. Kim et al. Nature 2023 paper shows quantum computer produces results classical methods cannot efficiently verify. CLOPS benchmark established.
2024
Heron r2 & Error Correction Break-Even
IBM Heron r2 (156Q) achieves 2Q gate fidelity >99.9%. Distance-5 surface code logical error rate below physical rate ("break-even"). NIST post-quantum standards finalised (Kyber, Dilithium).
2025
Flamingo — Multi-Chip Quantum Systems
IBM Flamingo processor enables communication between quantum chips via quantum interconnects. First modular quantum system. ~10 error-corrected logical qubits demonstrated.
2026
Kookaburra — 4,158 Qubits
IBM Kookaburra: 3-chip system (1,386Q × 3). First ~100 logical qubit demonstrations. Quantum-centric supercomputing paradigm — tightly coupled classical HPC + quantum. This event: QFF 2026!
2028–2029
Early Fault-Tolerant Applications
~1,000 logical qubits. First demonstrated advantage in molecular simulation (active pharmaceutical ingredients). Quantum network repeaters deployed in metropolitan areas. Post-quantum migration complete in US Federal infrastructure.
2033
100,000-Qubit Fault-Tolerant System
IBM target: 100,000 physical qubits → ~100 fully error-corrected logical qubits at 10⁻¹⁰ logical error rate. First fault-tolerant quantum chemistry: FeMo-co nitrogen fixation simulation. Shor's algorithm for RSA-2048 within reach.

Only 6 of the world's 195 countries have published national quantum strategies. Africa, most of Southeast Asia, and Latin America have near-zero quantum hardware. Unless deliberate action is taken — free cloud access, language-localised education, technology transfer — quantum computing will deepen global inequality before it reduces it. 📝 10. Day 5 Reflection & Final Assessment

Q1. Looking back at all 5 days — what is the single most important insight you gained about the relationship between quantum computing and global humanitarian challenges?
Open-ended reflection question. Strong answers might address: the timeline realism gap (NISQ vs fault-tolerant), the quantum literacy divide as the most immediate barrier, or a specific application (drug discovery, nitrogen fixation, post-quantum cryptography) that inspired them most.
Q2. What is "quantum-centric supercomputing" and how does it differ from standalone quantum computing?
✅ Answer: Quantum-centric supercomputing (QCS) is IBM's architectural vision where quantum processors are tightly integrated as accelerators within classical HPC infrastructure — similar to how GPUs accelerate classical AI training. In QCS, quantum processors handle computationally hard subroutines (molecular Hamiltonians, optimisation oracles) while classical hardware handles data I/O, pre/post-processing, and optimisation loops. This hybrid paradigm makes practical quantum advantage achievable sooner than standalone quantum computing because it leverages the best of both technologies.
Q3. A team in your track won the Impact Award but placed 4th on technical merit. Argue whether this is the right outcome.
Open-ended ethical reasoning question. Strong arguments acknowledge: technical excellence without impact is less valuable than impactful work with reasonable technical quality; however, incentivising low technical quality in the name of impact could undermine the credibility of quantum solutions; balance requires ensuring both technical and impact standards are high, and that separate awards (technical prize vs impact award) celebrate both dimensions without diminishing either.
